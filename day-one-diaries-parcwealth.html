Day One Diaries

Parc Wealth

Financial Advice, Finance & Insurance  ·  July 2026

Three businesses under one roof — financial advice, mortgage broking and insurance broking — with an AFSL to protect and a team whose time is worth far more on judgement calls than on formatting. Understandably careful about AI, and with the most to gain from getting it right.

The brief

Efficiency creation, in every corner of the business.

As PC put it when we sat down to start: "there's efficiency creation in so many parts of this business — that's what I'm interested in." That was the brief, and it shaped the whole two days.

Like most firms this size, they're already deep in Microsoft 365 and Copilot, and understandably protective of it — client data doesn't leave their environment, full stop.

So we set the rule that shaped everything: prototype and think things through with dummy data — then rebuild the finished approach back inside their own Microsoft world, where the real data lives. No compromise on security, no slowdown on capability.
The morning

Getting to first base.

We started with fundamentals — how instructions actually work, what a project remembers and what it doesn't, how to hand over just enough context without overloading it.

Michelle picked it up fast. By late morning she wasn't just following along, she was building — writing her own instruction set for a real piece of Parc's compliance work, and watching the output sharpen as she anchored it to the framework it needed to satisfy.

The afternoon

Two agents, live, by 4pm.

This is the part I'm proudest of. In one afternoon, working side by side across two platforms, we built and deployed two working tools the team could start using straight away:

  • A content compliance checker — running new material past their own internal standards before it goes out, saving many hours of painstaking checking before it gets to a human in the team.
  • A document-audit tool — Michelle had actually built the first version of this herself months earlier, her first real effort inside Copilot. We refined it together and had it live before long, checking a category of client documents against the rules they need to satisfy.

We tested the audit tool against a full set of test documents. What used to take days and an external review came back in seconds, with a clear read on what was fine and what needed a second look from a team member. A neat tool that helps the team, saves time, but still leaves the judgement call where it belongs.

By the time P.C. joined us at the end of the day to see it running, both tools were live, tested, and already shared with the team.

Michelle, Mark and P.C. at the end of Day One.

"Now you can drop a document in, and it comes straight back and tells you what's fine and what needs another look."

— Michelle Bryant, COO
What's next

Where it goes from here.

We're regrouping in a few days — giving Michelle time to think through what the highest-value projects could be next — before we reconvene ahead of Day Two. There's already a shortlist forming of where AI could help most.

"I learnt so much and I'm really excited about the next phase of the journey. It was a brilliant day and I've come away buzzing with ideas."

— Michelle Bryant, COO

One day in, and the team has two working tools they built themselves, understand, and trust. That's the bar for this work: not a demo, something that survives Monday morning without me in the room.
